# FinTrack 30-Day Launch Plan

## Goal

Reach the first 1,000 relevant installs while learning which message produces retained users—not just clicks.

Primary audience: English-speaking Android users in the United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Australia, and New Zealand who want a private, manual expense tracker without connecting a bank account.

Core promise: **See where your money goes without giving your money data away.**

## Before launch day

1. Wait until the current Google Play review is approved and verify the public listing on two Android devices.
2. Fix `https://cyfur.in/fintrack`, which currently returns a not-found page. The page should contain the five premium screenshots, three product benefits, privacy details, and one Google Play button.
3. Record one 20–30 second vertical demo: add an expense, open Insights, then show Backup & Restore.
4. Recruit 10–20 real early users. Ask for usability feedback first; ask for an honest Play review only after they have used the app.
5. Create one tagged Play URL per channel so Play Console can attribute traffic:
   - LinkedIn: `utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=fintrack_launch`
   - X: `utm_source=x&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=fintrack_launch`
   - Reddit: `utm_source=reddit&utm_medium=community&utm_campaign=fintrack_launch`
   - Product Hunt: `utm_source=producthunt&utm_medium=launch&utm_campaign=fintrack_launch`

Base URL: `https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.zenspend.app`

## Launch week

### Day 1 — Founder launch

- Publish the founder story on LinkedIn and X.
- Post the demo as an Instagram Reel and YouTube Short.
- Send the listing to friends, colleagues, and beta users with a request for feedback—not a forced five-star rating.
- Reply to every question and bug report within the day.

### Day 2 — Show the problem

Post: “Most finance apps ask for your bank login. FinTrack does not.” Demonstrate local storage, optional app lock, and export/restore.

### Day 3 — Show the speed

Post a 10–15 second recording of adding an expense and choosing a category. Caption: “Logged before the receipt leaves your hand.”

### Day 4 — Show the insight

Post the category chart and monthly summary. Explain one real spending decision the chart can support without promising financial outcomes.

### Day 5 — Community launch

- Share on Product Hunt from a personal maker account.
- Post a build story on Indie Hackers.
- Share in relevant Android, personal-finance, privacy, and maker communities only where self-promotion is allowed.
- Lead with the problem solved and lessons learned; do not paste the same promotional message everywhere.

### Days 6–7 — Proof and iteration

- Publish the best user quote only with permission and attribution.
- Fix the most repeated onboarding issue.
- Share a “week one: what users taught me” post.

## Weeks 2–4 content rhythm

Publish four useful pieces per week:

- One 15-second feature demo.
- One privacy or financial-habit education post.
- One founder/build-in-public post.
- One user-feedback or product-improvement post.

Use this content mix: 70% useful education, 20% product demonstration, 10% direct install request.

## Measurement

Check these every Monday in Play Console:

- Store listing visitors and unique install clicks by country.
- Click-through rate compared with the Finance peer benchmark.
- Traffic split by UTM source and campaign.
- Device acquisitions and uninstall trend.
- Android vitals and crash-free users.
- Rating themes and repeated support questions.

Do not buy broad ads until at least 100 relevant listing visits reveal the organic conversion rate. Then test one small country/audience at a time. Run a Play Store listing experiment only after enough traffic exists to reach statistical significance.

## 30-day decision rules

- High impressions, low clicks: test the icon, first screenshot, and short description.
- Good clicks, low installs: improve trust signals, privacy explanation, and first screenshots.
- Good installs, weak retention: improve first-run guidance and reminders before spending on acquisition.
- Strong retention and reviews: scale the winning channel and add localized English listings for en-GB and other priority markets.
